← Back to Corrente How costs work

Every circuit's cost, by where its power came from.

Your Basis switchboard measures every circuit in your home. Corrente allocates your solar and battery to those circuits, in proportion to what each one drew at the time. The result is a more realistic cost for every circuit.

The problem

Your switchboard can't tell solar from grid

Behind a backup gateway, solar, battery, and grid all reach your switchboard as one supply. So the switchboard prices every kilowatt-hour at your plan's time-of-use rates, as if it all came from the grid.

Proportional allocation

Allocated in proportion

For each interval, Corrente works out how much of your home's load your solar and battery covered. Each circuit gets that same share of what it drew. A circuit drawing a third of the load at midday gets a third of the solar.

By source

Split by source, not just by time

Each interval is split by where the power came from. The chart shows it: grid when you're importing, solar when the roof carries the load, battery when it's discharging.

Amortised solar & battery

Solar and battery aren't free

Enter your own amortised cost per kWh for solar and battery, worked out however you like. For example: install cost, plus a replacement inverter at around 10 years, divided by expected generation over 20 years. You get an honest running cost instead of treating your own energy as free.

Two numbers that matter

Estimated bill cost and savings, per circuit

Bill Cost estimates what your retailer charges for that circuit: only the grid kWh, at your plan's peak, shoulder, and off-peak rates. Savings is what you avoided paying by using your own solar and battery. Together they show what a circuit costs, and what your system does for it.

Worked example

A day on one circuit

The screen alongside shows one circuit, an EV charger, on Wednesday 5 August. It charges overnight on off-peak power, then tops up at midday while the solar is generating.

25 kWh of off-peak grid at $4.96, 8.6 kWh of solar amortised at $0.77, and 351 Wh of battery at $0.14. The result: a bill cost of $4.96, and $3.58 saved.

Everything in the switchboard

Hot water, heat pump, oven, lighting

The same allocation runs on every circuit, over any period: day, week, month, or year. So you can see which appliance is really expensive to run, and which one your solar already pays for.
